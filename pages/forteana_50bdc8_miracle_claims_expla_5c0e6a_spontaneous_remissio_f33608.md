## Introduction

Rare recoveries occupy a unique place in debates about miracle healing because they force the same basic question every time: did something truly impossible happen, or did medicine encounter an uncommon natural event that it cannot yet fully explain? The existence of spontaneous remission does not prove that miracle claims are false, but it does mean that an unexpected recovery cannot, by itself, demonstrate supernatural intervention. Equally, the fact that a remission is medically unexplained does not mean it has no natural cause. This tension makes spontaneous remission one of the strongest sceptical tests applied to healing claims, especially in Fortean discussions where extraordinary recoveries are often presented as evidence of divine or paranormal action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8271173/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCThe spontaneous remission of cancer: Current insights</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by G Radha · 2021 · Cited by 129 — Spontaneous remission (SR) of cancer is a rare but well-documented phenomenon. ## Why spontaneous remission complicates miracle claims

In medicine, spontaneous remission refers to the partial or complete disappearance of a disease without treatment, or after treatment considered insufficient to account for the improvement. The term is most commonly discussed in cancer, although unexpected remissions have also been reported in autoimmune disorders, infectious diseases and some neurological conditions. These events are rare, but they are well documented enough that physicians accept they occur, even if the biological mechanisms remain uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8271173/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCThe spontaneous remission of cancer: Current insights</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by G Radha · 2021 · Cited by 129 — Spontaneous remission (SR) of cancer is a rare but well-documented phenomenon. Mechanisms of SR are...</span></span></span>

For investigators of miracle claims, this creates an important logical challenge. If a disease occasionally improves without a known medical explanation, then any claimed miraculous cure must first rule out the possibility that it belongs to this small but genuine category of natural recoveries.

The sceptical argument is therefore not that spontaneous remission explains every miracle claim. Rather, it demonstrates that surprising recoveries are part of the natural history of disease. Before invoking supernatural intervention, investigators ask whether the case is exceptional because:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the original diagnosis was wrong;
* the disease naturally fluctuated;
* an earlier treatment worked after an unexpected delay;
* the recovery represents a genuine spontaneous remission;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_remission" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spontaneous remission</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spontaneous remission</span></span></span>
* or the recovery remains unexplained even after careful investigation.

</div>

Only after these alternatives have been examined does the question of a miracle become meaningful.

## The role of diagnosis, treatment and timing

The strongest healing claims depend less on the recovery itself than on the quality of the medical documentation surrounding it.

### Was the disease correctly diagnosed?

Many apparent [miracles]({{ 'miracles/' | relative_url }}) have later proved to involve diagnostic uncertainty. Imaging may have been misinterpreted, laboratory results may have been incomplete, or later knowledge may reveal that the patient never had the disease originally believed to be present.

Because of this, sceptical investigators place enormous weight on records made before any claimed healing occurred. Biopsy results, pathology reports and repeated clinical examinations generally provide stronger evidence than retrospective testimony alone.

### Could treatment have worked after all?

Another frequent complication is delayed therapeutic effect.

A patient may have received surgery, antibiotics, chemotherapy, radiotherapy or immune-based treatment weeks or months before the apparent miracle. If the disease subsequently disappears after prayer or pilgrimage, believers may naturally associate the timing with the religious event. Physicians, however, must also consider whether the earlier treatment produced a slower-than-expected response.

Modern cancer therapy has reinforced this caution. Some immunotherapies can trigger delayed tumour regression long after treatment begins, making simple chronological associations unreliable evidence of causation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8271173/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCThe spontaneous remission of cancer: Current insights</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by G Radha · 2021 · Cited by 129 — Spontaneous remission (SR) of cancer is a rare but well-documented phenomenon. ### Does timing create a false impression?

Human beings naturally connect dramatic events occurring close together.

If recovery follows a prayer service, blessing or visit to a shrine, it is psychologically compelling to treat one event as causing the other. Yet spontaneous remission demonstrates that recoveries sometimes occur unexpectedly even without any religious intervention. The mere sequence of "prayer followed by recovery" therefore cannot distinguish miracle from coincidence.

This does not disprove religious interpretations, but it means timing alone cannot establish them.

## What medicine knows about spontaneous remission

Although spontaneous remission remains uncommon, researchers have proposed several mechanisms that may contribute to it in at least some diseases.

The immune system is the leading candidate. Numerous documented cancer regressions have occurred after severe infections accompanied by high fever, suggesting that unusually intense immune activation may occasionally recognise and destroy malignant cells. This historical [observation]({{ 'observation/' | relative_url }}) helped inspire later research into cancer immunotherapy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8271173/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCThe spontaneous remission of cancer: Current insights</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by G Radha · 2021 · Cited by 129 — Spontaneous remission (SR) of cancer is a rare but well-documented phenomenon. Other proposed contributors include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* changes in tumour blood supply;
* programmed cell death within cancer cells;
* hormonal changes;
* alterations in the tumour microenvironment;
* unknown genetic or immune interactions.

</div>

Importantly, none of these mechanisms explains every documented remission. Researchers generally regard spontaneous remission as a descriptive label rather than a single disease process. It identifies an unexpected outcome without claiming a single universal cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8271173/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCThe spontaneous remission of cancer: Current insights</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by G Radha · 2021 · Cited by 129 — Spontaneous remission (SR) of cancer is a rare but well-documented phenomenon. ## How sceptics and believers read the same recovery

Perhaps the most interesting feature of spontaneous remission is that exactly the same medical event can support very different interpretations.

A sceptical investigator may argue:

<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_remission" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Spontaneous remission]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spontaneous remission</span></span></span> on is rare but real;
* unexplained does not mean supernatural;
* incomplete medical knowledge leaves room for future explanation.

A religious believer may instead argue:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the remission occurred immediately after prayer;
* similar recoveries cluster around particular shrines or devotions;
* divine action may work through natural biological processes rather than against them.

</div>

Neither interpretation changes the underlying medical facts. Instead, they differ over what counts as the best explanation once those facts have been established.

This distinction is especially important because medicine is generally cautious about declaring anything impossible. A physician may honestly conclude that a recovery is medically inexplicable while making no judgement about whether it was miraculous.

## Why recognised miracle investigations demand more than an unexplained cure

Formal investigations into miracle claims often incorporate spontaneous remission directly into their assessment procedures.

For example, investigations associated with healing claims at Lourdes require independent confirmation of the diagnosis, evidence that the illness was serious, complete and lasting recovery, and careful consideration of whether any recognised medical explanation—including spontaneous remission—could account for the improvement. Medical experts may conclude that a cure is scientifically unexplained, but the religious judgement that it constitutes a miracle belongs to ecclesiastical authorities rather than physicians.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lourdes_Medical_Bureau" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lourdes Medical Bureau</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lourdes Medical Bureau</span></span></span>

This separation reflects an important principle: medicine can evaluate whether known biological mechanisms adequately explain a recovery, but deciding whether an unexplained recovery represents divine intervention is a philosophical or theological judgement rather than a medical one.

## Why rare recovery remains the strongest sceptical test

Spontaneous remission neither proves nor disproves miracle healing. Instead, it establishes an essential baseline for evaluating extraordinary recovery claims.

If genuine natural recoveries occur without clear explanation, then every alleged miracle must demonstrate why it cannot belong to that category. The burden of proof therefore shifts from showing that a recovery was remarkable to showing that ordinary medical uncertainty has been excluded as far as possible.

For Fortean investigations, this makes spontaneous remission especially significant. It reminds investigators that medicine still contains rare, poorly understood recoveries, while also showing why extraordinary claims require documentation far stronger than the surprising recovery alone. The mystery lies not in whether unexpected cures happen—they demonstrably do—but in determining which recoveries reflect unusual biology, which expose gaps in medical knowledge, and whether any truly require an explanation beyond nature itself.
