## Introduction

Public imagery released by the U.S. Department of Defense's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) has become one of the most visible pieces of evidence in modern UAP debate. Yet the most important lesson from these images is not that they reveal extraordinary objects. Rather, they show how difficult it can be to identify distant targets from limited sensor data, and why an "unresolved" label is not the same as evidence for something exotic. This distinction sits at the heart of the difference between UFOlogy and a Fortean approach. Instead of treating uncertainty as proof of hidden technologies or extraterrestrial craft, the evidence encourages careful attention to ambiguity, incomplete information and the limits of [observation]({{ 'observation/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO+2NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Official UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP ImageryForward-Looking Infrared video footage of an unidentified object recorded by the aircrew of a U.S. Navy F/A-18F in 2015. &quot;...</span></span></span>

## Resolved images and ordinary explanations

AARO's public image library deliberately mixes unresolved material with cases that have been investigated and explained. This is important because it demonstrates how easily ordinary objects can appear remarkable when viewed through military infrared sensors, stabilised optics or long-range cameras.

One of the clearest examples is the famous **[GOFAST]({{ 'gofast/' | relative_url }})** infrared video recorded by a U.S. Navy aircraft. For years it circulated as evidence of an object racing just above the ocean. After analysing the aircraft's geometry, sensor data and viewing angles, AARO concluded that the apparent speed was largely a result of parallax: the object was much farther away than many viewers assumed and therefore moving far more slowly than it first appeared. The office did not identify the precise object, but it rejected the dramatic interpretation suggested by the video's appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO+2U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Official UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP ImageryForward-Looking Infrared video footage of an unidentified object recorded by the aircrew of a U.S. Navy F/A-18F in 2015. &quot;...</span></span></span>

The broader pattern is even more instructive than any individual case. In its annual reporting and public briefings, AARO has repeatedly stated that it has resolved hundreds of reports as familiar objects, including:

* birds;
* balloons;
* drones;
* satellites;
* conventional aircraft.

Many additional cases are expected to close in the same way once final reviews are complete. This demonstrates an important feature of visual evidence. Human observers naturally interpret isolated images according to expectations. A bright point can appear to manoeuvre because the camera moves. Infrared glare can distort an object's apparent shape. Compression artefacts, autofocus changes and incomplete metadata all make confident identification harder than casual viewing suggests. A striking image is therefore not necessarily a reliable guide to what actually occupied the sky.

## Why some cases stay uncertain

An unresolved case is often misunderstood as an unexplained phenomenon. AARO uses the term much more narrowly.

In official practice, a report may remain unresolved because investigators lack enough reliable information to determine which [ordinary explanation]({{ 'ordinary-causes/' | relative_url }}) is correct. Missing radar tracks, incomplete flight records, poor image quality, short observation times or absent sensor calibration can prevent analysts from distinguishing between several plausible possibilities. The result is uncertainty rather than confirmation of anything extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO+2NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Official UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP ImageryForward-Looking Infrared video footage of an unidentified object recorded by the aircrew of a U.S. Navy F/A-18F in 2015. &quot;...</span></span></span>

This distinction appears repeatedly in AARO's public releases. Some newly released videos remain unresolved because the available imagery is simply insufficient for a confident conclusion. In one publicly released European case, AARO explicitly stated that the footage did not provide enough information to render a determination. Another infrared video was assessed as depicting a genuine physical object whose behaviour appeared unremarkable, making further analysis unnecessary despite the object's remaining unidentified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dvidshub.net/video/988675/pr-017-unresolved-uap-report-europe-2024" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dvidshub.net">[DVIDS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dvidshub.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PR-017, Unresolved UAP Report, Europe 2024The video footage associated with this report is insufficient for AARO to render a determinatio...</span></span></span>

## Why public imagery often carries more meaning than it deserves

Images released by government agencies inevitably acquire symbolic significance beyond their evidential value.

A short infrared clip can become a cultural icon because viewers fill gaps in the available information with assumptions. Online discussions often freeze around individual frames while overlooking factors such as camera tracking, aircraft motion, sensor limitations or missing contextual data. The visual impression becomes stronger than the evidential foundation.

Military imagery is especially vulnerable to this problem because it is rarely collected for scientific investigation. Targeting systems are designed to support operational missions rather than careful measurement of unknown objects. Important contextual information—including precise range, atmospheric conditions or complete sensor metadata—may never have been recorded or may remain classified. As NASA's study observed, these limitations make reliable interpretation difficult even for specialists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportThe study of Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) presents a unique scientific opportunity tha...</span></span></span>

The consequence is that the public often sees compelling imagery without the accompanying information needed for confident identification.

## How Forteana handles doubt without closure

Within a Fortean perspective, unresolved imagery is valuable precisely because it resists premature certainty.

A believer may interpret an unidentified object as evidence of non-human technology. A determined sceptic may insist that every unresolved image must eventually prove mundane. [Forteana]({{ 'forteana/' | relative_url }}) occupies a less comfortable position. It accepts that some reports remain uncertain because the evidence is genuinely incomplete, while recognising that history shows many apparently mysterious observations eventually acquire ordinary explanations.

AARO's imagery reinforces this cautious approach. The office's own archive contains examples where appearances proved misleading and many more where several conventional explanations remain plausible. Birds, balloons and aircraft continue to account for substantial numbers of investigated reports, even when initial impressions suggested something remarkable. At the same time, a smaller number of cases remain open not because they demonstrate extraordinary craft, but because available evidence cannot yet discriminate reliably between competing possibilities.
