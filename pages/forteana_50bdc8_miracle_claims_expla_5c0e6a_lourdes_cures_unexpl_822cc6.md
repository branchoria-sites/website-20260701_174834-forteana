## Introduction

Lourdes occupies a distinctive place in discussions of miracle claims because it deliberately separates two different questions: **has an unexpected recovery occurred that medicine cannot currently explain, and does that recovery therefore count as a miracle?** The answer to the first question is sought through medical investigation, while the second belongs to religious judgement. This distinction makes Lourdes one of the few settings where a major religious tradition has built a formal process intended to prevent every dramatic recovery from being immediately labelled miraculous.

For students of [Forteana]({{ 'forteana/' | relative_url }}), this distinction is especially important. Lourdes demonstrates that an event can remain medically unusual without automatically becoming public proof of divine intervention. The continuing debate is therefore not simply about whether remarkable cures occur, but about what level of evidence is required before an unexplained recovery can reasonably be interpreted as a miracle.

## How Lourdes separates medical review from religious judgement

Since the nineteenth century, people reporting cures at Lourdes have been encouraged to present themselves to the Sanctuary's Medical Bureau rather than simply leave with a personal testimony. The purpose is not to prove [miracles]({{ 'miracles/' | relative_url }}) but to document diagnoses, collect medical records, examine patients and determine whether a reported recovery withstands clinical scrutiny. Over time this process has become increasingly structured, involving the Lourdes Medical Bureau and, for the most difficult cases, the International Medical Committee of Lourdes (CMIL), whose members include specialists from different countries and medical disciplines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lourdes-france.org/en/medical-bureau-sanctuary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lourdes-france.org">[Lourdes France+2Lourdes France]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lourdes-france.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lourdes FranceThe Medical Bureau of the SanctuaryThe members of the International Medical Committee of Lourdes have the task of assessing...</span></span></span>

A crucial feature of the system is that the medical investigators do **not** declare miracles. Their role is limited to deciding whether a cure is medically inexplicable according to current scientific knowledge. Only after that stage can the bishop responsible for the healed person's home diocese decide whether the event should be recognised by the Catholic Church as miraculous. That ecclesiastical decision may take into account spiritual testimony and wider pastoral considerations in addition to the medical findings. A medically unexplained cure therefore does not automatically become an official miracle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4313433/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Lourdes France]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCLourdes: A uniquely Catholic approach to medicine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIHby TJ Dichoso · 2015 · Cited by 5 — According to the Lambertini criteria, the disease must be serious and impossible to cure by...</span></span></span>

This institutional separation matters because it prevents medicine from being asked to answer theological questions while also preventing theology from bypassing medical examination.

## What makes a cure rapid, complete and lasting?

The Lourdes process is intentionally conservative. Cases are expected to satisfy criteria derived from the eighteenth-century work of Prospero Lambertini, later Pope Benedict XIV, which continue to influence Catholic evaluations of alleged miracles.

Among the principal expectations are:

* the original diagnosis must be well established;
* the illness must have been serious, with little realistic prospect of spontaneous recovery;
* the recovery must be rapid or sudden rather than gradual in the expected course of treatment;
* the recovery must be complete, without significant remaining disability;
* the improvement must persist over many years rather than prove temporary;
* no conventional medical explanation should adequately account for the outcome.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4313433/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Lourdes France]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCLourdes: A uniquely Catholic approach to medicine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIHby TJ Dichoso · 2015 · Cited by 5 — According to the Lambertini criteria, the disease must be serious and impossible to cure by...</span></span></span>

Long-term follow-up is therefore essential. Some investigations continue for many years before any conclusion is reached because relapses, revised diagnoses or new medical knowledge may alter how a case is understood. A dramatic improvement observed over days or weeks is not considered sufficient by itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4313433/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Lourdes France]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCLourdes: A uniquely Catholic approach to medicine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIHby TJ Dichoso · 2015 · Cited by 5 — According to the Lambertini criteria, the disease must be serious and impossible to cure by...</span></span></span>

## Why so few reported healings become recognised miracles

The contrast between reported cures and recognised miracles illustrates how demanding the process has become. Thousands of healings have been reported since the [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}) associated with Lourdes, yet only a small fraction have ultimately been recognised by the Catholic Church as miracles after medical and ecclesiastical review. The large difference reflects strict filtering rather than an assumption that every testimony is either true or false.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lourdes-france.org/en/the-miracles-of-lourdes/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lourdes-france.org">[Lourdes France+2Lourdes France]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lourdes-france.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lourdes FranceLes miracles de LourdesMore than 80% of the cures recognised as miraculous concern women. Historical studies also show that standards have tightened considerably over time. Earlier investigations often relied on limited documentation, whereas twentieth-century and modern cases increasingly incorporate imaging, laboratory testing, specialist review and prolonged [observation]({{ 'observation/' | relative_url }}). As diagnostic methods improved, the proportion of claims judged medically inexplicable fell sharply. This trend reflects both better medicine and greater caution rather than evidence that unusual recoveries have disappeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3854941/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Lourdes Medical Cures Revisited - PMC - NIHby B François · 2012 · Cited by 53 — In the six years from 1933 through 1938, 48 cures...</span></span></span>

## Why unexplained recovery is not the same as proof

Perhaps the most important lesson from Lourdes is epistemological rather than theological. "Unexplained" and "miraculous" are not equivalent statements.

An unexplained recovery may reflect:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* incomplete understanding of the disease;
* uncertainty about the original diagnosis;
* an exceptionally rare spontaneous remission;
* interactions between treatment and biological processes that are not fully understood;
* psychological or physiological mechanisms that remain difficult to measure.

</div>

Medicine has repeatedly revised its understanding of diseases once thought impossible to survive. Consequently, medical investigators at Lourdes define their conclusions in deliberately limited terms: they judge whether current medical knowledge adequately explains the recovery, not whether supernatural intervention has been demonstrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4313433/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCLourdes: A uniquely Catholic approach to medicine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIHby TJ Dichoso · 2015 · Cited by 5 — According to the Lambertini criteria, the disease must be serious and impossible to cure by...</span></span></span>

The Catholic Church likewise avoids claiming that every medically unexplained cure is miraculous. Even after favourable medical findings, bishops may decline to issue a formal declaration of miracle. This layered process acknowledges that theological interpretation involves different standards and questions from clinical investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lourdes-france.org/en/recognition-miracle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lourdes-france.org">[Lourdes France]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lourdes-france.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lourdes FranceRecognition of a miracleThe support of the Lambertini criteria will assure that the cure has been found complete and lastin...</span></span></span>

## What sceptics and supporters each find persuasive

Lourdes remains a point of disagreement because the same evidence supports different interpretations.

Supporters argue that the value of Lourdes lies precisely in its reluctance to proclaim miracles. They note that independent physicians, including doctors with no religious commitment, may participate in reviewing cases, and they view the extensive documentation and long-term follow-up as evidence of institutional caution rather than credulity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4313433/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCLourdes: A uniquely Catholic approach to medicine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIHby TJ Dichoso · 2015 · Cited by 5 — According to the Lambertini criteria, the disease must be serious and impossible to cure by...</span></span></span>

Sceptics respond that medically unexplained outcomes are expected to occur occasionally in large populations, especially when diagnoses, disease mechanisms and spontaneous remissions are imperfectly understood. They also argue that improvements in historical medical documentation have not always matched modern standards and that some older cases remain difficult to reassess using contemporary evidence. Critics therefore maintain that the absence of a medical explanation should not be treated as positive evidence for supernatural causation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3854941/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Lourdes Medical Cures Revisited - PMC - NIHby B François · 2012 · Cited by 53 — In the six years from 1933 through 1938, 48 cures...</span></span></span>

Interestingly, both perspectives often agree on one point: a surprising recovery deserves careful investigation before strong conclusions are drawn.

## Why Lourdes remains significant in Fortean discussions

Within the broader study of extraordinary claims, Lourdes is notable not because it claims every healing is miraculous, but because it institutionalises [doubt]({{ 'doubt/' | relative_url }}) alongside belief. The system is designed to eliminate weak cases rather than multiply them, distinguishing between personal testimony, medical anomaly and religious interpretation.

That makes Lourdes an unusual example in Forteana. Instead of presenting unexplained events as self-evident proof of the supernatural, it illustrates how an event may remain genuinely unusual while still resisting definitive explanation. The enduring question is therefore not simply whether remarkable recoveries occur—they clearly do—but whether the gap between current medical knowledge and complete explanation can ever, by itself, establish that a miracle has taken place.
