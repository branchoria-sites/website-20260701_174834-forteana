## Introduction

NASA's work on unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) represents an important shift in how unusual aerial reports are framed. Rather than treating every unexplained sighting as evidence of something extraordinary, or dismissing all reports as [mistakes]({{ 'mistakes/' | relative_url }}), the agency argues that the central problem is scientific: there is too little reliable data. The quality of evidence is weakened by uncalibrated sensors, incomplete metadata, inconsistent reporting and, crucially, a longstanding stigma that discourages people from documenting what they observe. This reframing is significant within the broader Fortean debate because it separates the act of reporting from the interpretation of the event. A witness may have seen something genuinely unidentified without that implying an exotic explanation. NASA's proposed remedy is therefore not belief, but better governance of [observation]({{ 'observation/' | relative_url }}) through standardised reporting, improved instrumentation and a scientific culture that distinguishes curiosity from credulity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

## Why ridicule damages reporting quality

One of the strongest conclusions of NASA's 2023 Independent Study Team is that social attitudes can become a scientific obstacle. The report argues that many potential witnesses, particularly pilots and other professionals, hesitate to report unusual observations because they fear ridicule, damage to their careers or association with sensational UFO claims. This reluctance creates a selection problem: potentially useful observations never enter the record, while the reports that do emerge often arrive years later with diminished evidential value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

This is an important distinction. NASA does not claim that witnesses are necessarily correct about what they observed. Instead, it argues that suppressing reports prevents investigators from collecting the information needed to determine [ordinary explanations]({{ 'ordinary-causes/' | relative_url }}). Scientific inquiry depends upon observations being recorded before they are interpreted.

The agency therefore recommends treating UAP reporting more like aviation safety reporting than paranormal testimony. In aviation, near misses, equipment failures and unexpected events are valuable precisely because they reveal gaps in knowledge. A similar reporting culture for unidentified observations would increase the amount of usable information without assuming any particular cause. Media coverage of the report highlighted this recommendation, noting NASA's emphasis on reducing the "tin-foil hat" stereotype attached to unexplained aerial sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science+2Sky News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

## What calibrated sensors can add

NASA's report repeatedly stresses that most current UAP evidence is scientifically weak not because it is mysterious, but because it was never collected for scientific investigation.

Many reports rely upon:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* cameras that were not calibrated for precise measurement;
* single viewpoints with no independent confirmation;
* missing information about camera settings, location or timing;
* absent environmental data;
* incomplete archiving of the original recordings.

</div>

Without these details, investigators often cannot distinguish between a genuine external object, an optical illusion, an atmospheric effect or a sensor artefact. The report therefore frames unidentified phenomena primarily as a data-quality problem rather than an anomaly problem.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

NASA recommends using multiple well-calibrated sensors whenever possible. Combining optical imagery with infrared observations, radar, satellite information and accurate metadata allows investigators to cross-check competing explanations instead of relying upon a single ambiguous video.

The report also points out that careful analysis has already shown some apparent UAP to be artefacts created by sensor limitations or processing effects. Better calibration does not merely help discover unusual events—it also helps eliminate false anomalies more quickly, improving confidence in the cases that remain unexplained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/NASA_Unidentified_Anomalous_Phenomena%3A_Independent_Study_Team_Report/Responses_to_Statement_of_Task" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Responses to Statement of Task</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena: Independent...14 Oct 2023 — Indeed, several apparent UAP have been demonstrated to be s...</span></span></span>

## How to separate witnesses from explanations

Perhaps the report's most useful conceptual contribution is its insistence that observation and explanation should be evaluated independently.

A witness can be:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* sincere;
* experienced;
* careful;
* still mistaken about the cause of what they observed.

</div>

Conversely, a report may deserve careful investigation even if its eventual explanation proves entirely ordinary.

This distinction addresses a common weakness in public discussion. UFO debates frequently collapse into two opposing assumptions:

* if the witness appears credible, the extraordinary explanation must also be credible;
* if the explanation is ordinary, the witness must have been dishonest or foolish.

NASA rejects both positions. Instead, it recommends preserving observations while remaining agnostic about their interpretation until sufficient evidence is available. This approach allows investigators to respect witnesses without granting evidential weight to conclusions that exceed the available data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

For Fortean questions, this distinction is especially important. Many historical controversies have become polarised because criticism of an explanation was perceived as criticism of the observer. NASA's framework attempts to separate those issues.

## A governance problem rather than a belief problem

Within the wider discussion of [scientific dismissal]({{ 'dismissal/' | relative_url }}), NASA's report treats stigma as an institutional design problem rather than a psychological curiosity.

Its recommendations include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* normalised reporting channels;
* standardised data collection;
* transparent evaluation methods;
* use of artificial intelligence and machine-learning tools to identify conventional objects before highlighting genuine anomalies;
* collaboration across civilian, commercial and government data sources.

</div>

The objective is not to prove extraordinary claims but to improve the quality of evidence entering scientific analysis. NASA explicitly states that its study found no evidence that reported UAP are extraterrestrial in origin. Equally, it argues that poor evidence should not be confused with evidence that nothing unusual was observed. Unknown simply means that the available information is insufficient for confident identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science+2NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

## Why this matters for Forteana

[Charles Fort]({{ 'charles-fort/' | relative_url }}) criticised institutions for allowing social assumptions to determine which observations deserved attention. NASA's UAP study does not embrace Fort's conclusions, but it does acknowledge a related institutional risk: ridicule can distort the evidence base before scientific analysis even begins.

Its proposed solution remains firmly within mainstream scientific practice. Instead of lowering evidential standards, NASA argues for raising them through better reporting systems, calibrated instruments, richer metadata and transparent evaluation. In that sense, the agency reframes one of [Forteana]({{ 'forteana/' | relative_url }})'s oldest questions. The issue is not whether extraordinary explanations should be accepted more readily, but whether potentially valuable observations should be recorded more consistently before they are accepted or rejected.

That distinction preserves scientific scepticism while recognising that premature dismissal can itself become a source of ignorance.
