## Introduction

Animal-fall reports are easiest to assess when investigators treat them as time-sensitive field observations rather than as ready-made mysteries. The central question is not whether witnesses genuinely found fish, frogs or other animals where they seemed unexpected, but how those animals arrived there. The first hours after discovery are crucial because rain, scavengers, traffic, clean-up and repeated retelling can rapidly erase or distort the original evidence. A careful investigation separates observed facts from proposed explanations and tests each explanation against the physical evidence, local [weather]({{ 'weather/' | relative_url }}), species involved and surrounding landscape. This approach fits both scientific practice and the Fortean tradition of documenting unusual events without assuming either a paranormal or a mundane answer in advance. Guidance from wildlife health investigators also shows the importance of recording location, condition and context before specimens deteriorate or are moved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.usgs.gov/centers/nwhc/science/field-manual-wildlife-diseases" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: usgs.gov">[USGS+2U.S. Geological Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">usgs.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Field Manual of Wildlife Diseases &#124; U.S</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geological SurveyJan 1, 2021 — The purpose of this manual is to provide information that will enhance the ecological understanding and co...</span></span></span>

## Record the exact scene before anything changes

The first task is to preserve information, not to explain it. Even a convincing-looking animal fall can become impossible to evaluate if the original scene is poorly documented.

Record immediately:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The exact date and local time.
* GPS coordinates or a precise address.
* The weather at the time of discovery and during the preceding 24 hours.
* Whether rain was still falling or had already stopped.
* The approximate number of animals present.
* Whether they were concentrated in one patch, spread over a line, or scattered widely.
* The surfaces on which they were found, such as roofs, pavements, grass, parked vehicles or open fields.

</div>

Photographs should be taken before animals are touched or collected. Wide-angle photographs establish the overall distribution, while close-up images document individual animals and surrounding features such as drains, gutters, ponds, streams, roofs or nearby trees.

Witnesses should be asked to distinguish clearly between what they directly observed and what they inferred. "I found fish after heavy rain" is an [observation]({{ 'observation/' | relative_url }}). "The fish fell from the clouds" is a hypothesis that requires independent evidence.

## Identify the animals and their condition

Species identification often narrows the range of plausible explanations more effectively than dramatic witness testimony.

Useful questions include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Are all animals the same species or are several species mixed together?
* Are they similar in size and age?
* Are they freshwater, marine or terrestrial species?
* Are they common in nearby ponds, rivers or wetlands?
* Are they alive, freshly dead or decomposing?

</div>

Uniformity can be informative. Reports involving many similarly sized juvenile fish, for example, are more consistent with a local source than a random collection from different habitats. Conversely, a mixture of unrelated species may suggest flooding, dumping or another transport mechanism.

Condition also matters. Investigators should note visible injuries, missing scales or limbs, signs of bird predation, drying, mud deposits or crushing by vehicles. ## Compare the report with recent weather

Weather evidence should be gathered independently rather than relying solely on [witness memory]({{ 'witness-memory/' | relative_url }}).

Check:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Thunderstorms or severe convective weather.
* Tornado or waterspout reports.
* Wind direction and estimated strength.
* Radar imagery where available.
* Rainfall totals.
* Local flooding or overflowing drainage systems.

</div>

The timing matters as much as the weather itself. If the reported discovery occurred hours after a storm had passed, alternative transport mechanisms may have operated between the storm and the discovery. Conversely, an event occurring during an intense storm with documented rotating weather systems deserves closer examination because storm-related lifting mechanisms have long been proposed for some animal-fall reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fisheries.noaa.gov">[NOAA Fisheries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fisheries.noaa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NOAA FisheriesNOAA Fisheries: Welcome to NOAANOAA Fisheries—the trusted government authority on the science and management of fish, other...</span></span></span>

## Look for nearby natural sources

Many apparently anomalous falls occur surprisingly close to ordinary sources.

Survey the surrounding area for:

* Ponds.
* Streams.
* Flooded fields.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* Temporary pools.
* Drainage channels.
* Fish farms.
* Reservoirs.
* Coastal waters, if relevant.

</div>

Measure approximate distances rather than estimating casually. A small pond only a few hundred metres away may prove more significant than a large lake several kilometres distant.

The ground itself should also be examined. Mud splashes, flood debris, fresh runoff channels or water marks on vegetation may indicate recent transport across the landscape rather than through the air.

## Test ordinary alternatives one by one

Rather than asking whether an event is mysterious, investigators should ask which explanation best fits all available evidence.

Possible alternatives include:

* **Storm transport.** Strong storm updraughts or [waterspouts]({{ 'waterspouts/' | relative_url }}) lifting small aquatic animals.
* **Flooding.** Overflowing water carrying animals onto roads, gardens or fields.
* **Overland movement.** Frogs and other amphibians naturally dispersing during wet weather.
* **Bird activity.** Predators dropping or regurgitating prey while flying.
* **Human causes.** Discarded bait, fish stocking, deliberate [hoaxes]({{ 'hoaxes/' | relative_url }}) or accidental spills.
* **Drainage systems.** Animals emerging from culverts, storm drains or overflowing sewers.

Each explanation should be tested against the observed distribution. For example, animals found only beside a flooded ditch support a different interpretation from animals reportedly discovered across rooftops in several streets.

## Evaluate the pattern of distribution

The spatial pattern often provides stronger evidence than the number of animals.

Questions worth documenting include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Does the distribution follow the direction of recent wind?
* Are animals concentrated in depressions where water collected?
* Are roofs affected as well as the ground?
* Are enclosed gardens involved?
* Are sheltered areas untouched while exposed surfaces contain animals?
* Does the distribution continue across property boundaries?

</div>

A genuine transport event should usually produce a pattern that reflects physical processes rather than arbitrary placement.

## Interview witnesses carefully

Witness interviews should be conducted as soon as possible, before stories become harmonised through conversation or media coverage.

Useful questions include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* When was the first animal noticed?
* Did anyone actually see animals descending?
* Was there hail, strong wind or unusual cloud structure?
* Who discovered the event first?
* Were photographs taken immediately?
* Were any animals removed before others arrived?

</div>

Independent accounts should be preserved separately. Agreement on unexpected details can strengthen a report, while contradictions may reveal how assumptions entered the narrative over time.

## Preserve specimens appropriately

Where collection is lawful and appropriate, representative specimens should be preserved carefully for later identification rather than gathering every animal indiscriminately.

Good practice includes:

* Wearing appropriate protective equipment.
* Recording exactly where each specimen was collected.
* Keeping specimens cool if laboratory examination may follow.
* Avoiding contamination between specimens.
* Labelling containers with location, time and collector.

Wildlife health guidance consistently emphasises that specimen value declines rapidly once decomposition, scavenging or poor handling begins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.usgs.gov/centers/nwhc/science/diagnostic-case-submission-guidelines" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: usgs.gov">[USGS+2USGS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">usgs.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">diagnostic case submission guidelines</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Diagnostic Case Submission GuidelinesJul 22, 2024 — Carcasses must be in good postmortem condition (i.e., intact carcass, no foul odo...</span></span></span>

## Common pitfalls that weaken an investigation

Several recurring [mistakes]({{ 'mistakes/' | relative_url }}) make animal-fall reports difficult to evaluate.

* Assuming that finding animals after rain proves they fell from the sky.
* Moving specimens before photographing the scene.
* Ignoring nearby ponds, drains or flood channels.
* Failing to identify the species.
* Relying on newspaper summaries instead of interviewing original witnesses.
* Treating later [retellings]({{ 'retellings/' | relative_url }}) as equivalent to contemporaneous observations.
* Allowing dramatic explanations to replace documented evidence.

The strongest investigations remain descriptive for as long as possible. They distinguish clearly between established observations, probable mechanisms and unresolved questions. Within the study of Forteana, this disciplined approach makes unusual animal-fall reports more valuable, whether they ultimately support an [ordinary explanation]({{ 'ordinary-causes/' | relative_url }}) or remain genuinely difficult to account for using the available evidence.
