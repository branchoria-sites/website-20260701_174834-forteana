## Introduction

Anomalistics asks a simple but demanding question: can strange claims be investigated seriously without assuming they are either true or false? Within the Fortean tradition, the answer is yes. Rather than treating unusual reports as proof of hidden forces or dismissing them as nonsense, anomalistics creates a disciplined framework for examining anomalies with ordinary scientific standards. Curiosity is encouraged, but it is restrained by evidence, critical testing and a willingness to change conclusions when better explanations emerge. This approach makes anomalistics a practical expression of suspended judgment: it keeps unusual reports open to investigation while preventing fascination from becoming certainty. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/anomalistics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaAnomalistics - Psi Encyclopedia13 Apr 2015 — Anomalistics is generally governed by the Mertonian norms of science: commun...</span></span></span>

## What anomalistics studies

Anomalistics is the systematic investigation of reports, observations and events that appear to fall outside current expectations or accepted explanations. The defining feature is not that the subject is paranormal, but that it is anomalous—something that resists immediate classification.

That distinction matters. A report of mysterious lights, an apparently haunted building, an unexpected atmospheric phenomenon or an unusual coincidence may all enter anomalistic study for the same reason: they require explanation, not endorsement. The field does not begin by assuming that conventional science is wrong, nor that witnesses are necessarily mistaken. Instead, it asks what combination of evidence, [observation]({{ 'observation/' | relative_url }}) and analysis can account for what was reported. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/anomalistics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaAnomalistics - Psi Encyclopedia13 Apr 2015 — Anomalistics is generally governed by the Mertonian norms of science: commun...</span></span></span>

Marcello Truzzi, one of the figures most closely associated with anomalistics, argued that the discipline occupies a middle ground between unquestioning belief and reflexive debunking. He described scepticism as an investigative attitude rather than a predetermined conclusion, emphasising that claims deserve proportionate scrutiny rather than automatic rejection. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/marcello-truzzi/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Marcello Truzzi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaMarcello Truzzi - Psi Encyclopedia27 May 2026 — Known for distinguishing open-minded scepticism from debunking, he edited...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2026</span></span></span>

## Why multiple disciplines matter

One reason anomalistics differs from many discussions of paranormal claims is that it assumes no single discipline can explain every anomaly.

A single case may require expertise from several fields:

* **Astronomy** to identify planets, meteors or rare atmospheric events mistaken for unidentified objects.
* **[Psychology]({{ 'psychology/' | relative_url }})** to understand perception, expectation, memory and eyewitness testimony.
* **Physics and engineering** to evaluate measurements, photographs, recordings or instrument behaviour.
* **Meteorology** to assess unusual [weather]({{ 'weather/' | relative_url }}) or optical effects.
* **Folklore and history** to understand how local traditions shape reports and interpretations.

The Psi Encyclopedia notes that an alleged UFO, for example, may ultimately require psychology, astronomy, aviation knowledge and physics rather than belonging exclusively to any one subject. The anomaly itself determines which expertise is needed, not the other way around. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/anomalistics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaAnomalistics - Psi Encyclopedia13 Apr 2015 — Anomalistics is generally governed by the Mertonian norms of science: commun...</span></span></span>

This multidisciplinary approach also reduces the temptation to force every case into a favourite explanation. Instead of asking whether an event is "really paranormal", investigators ask which evidence each discipline can contribute and where genuine uncertainty remains.

## Where Fortean curiosity meets sceptical method

The attraction of [Forteana]({{ 'forteana/' | relative_url }}) has always been its willingness to notice reports that conventional categories struggle to accommodate. Anomalistics preserves that curiosity while adding what might be called scientific brakes.

Those brakes consist of familiar research practices rather than special rules for extraordinary subjects:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* collecting original witness accounts where possible;
* preserving physical evidence before interpretation;
* comparing competing explanations rather than defending one preferred theory;
* recognising uncertainty when evidence is incomplete;
* remaining willing to abandon an attractive explanation if better evidence appears.

</div>

The Psi Encyclopedia characterises anomalistics as being guided by the ordinary norms of scientific inquiry, including [organised scepticism]({{ 'scepticism-b4393c/' | relative_url }}), disinterestedness and openness to original data. In other words, the methods are conventional even when the subject matter is unusual. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/anomalistics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaAnomalistics - Psi Encyclopedia13 Apr 2015 — Anomalistics is generally governed by the Mertonian norms of science: commun...</span></span></span>

This balance explains why anomalistics often frustrates both committed believers and determined debunkers. It rarely provides immediate certainty. Instead, it attempts to match confidence to the available evidence.

## Why psychology is part of the investigation, not a dismissal

One common misunderstanding is that introducing psychology automatically means dismissing witnesses. In practice, anomalistic psychology treats unusual experiences as genuine experiences while asking how they arose.

Researchers such as Chris French have focused on explaining apparently paranormal experiences through established psychological and physical mechanisms where possible. This includes work on perception, memory, suggestion, sleep phenomena, expectation and cognitive biases. None of these explanations require assuming that witnesses are dishonest or irrational. Ordinary mental processes operating under unusual circumstances can produce vivid and sincere reports. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gold.ac.uk/psychology/staff/french/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gold.ac.uk">[Goldsmiths, University of London]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gold.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">smiths, University of LondonChristopher FrenchChris is an expert in the area of anomalistic psychology, focusing upon non- paranormal...</span></span></span>

This perspective changes the investigative question. Rather than asking whether someone "made it up", anomalistic psychology asks what combination of environmental conditions and human cognition produced the reported [experience]({{ 'experience/' | relative_url }}).

That approach is especially valuable in Fortean investigations because many reports occur under poor observational conditions: darkness, surprise, emotional stress, ambiguous stimuli or long delays before recollection. Psychological analysis therefore complements, rather than replaces, physical investigation.

## What happens when no explanation is found?

Anomalistics does not require every case to reach a definite conclusion.

Some reports are eventually explained through [ordinary causes]({{ 'ordinary-causes/' | relative_url }}) that were initially overlooked. Others are exposed as frauds or misunderstandings. Some remain unresolved because key evidence was never collected, observations conflict or the event cannot be reproduced. A small remainder continue to resist straightforward explanation without necessarily demonstrating anything supernatural.

This willingness to leave cases unresolved is one of the discipline's defining strengths. Instead of filling evidential gaps with speculation, anomalistics accepts that uncertainty is sometimes the most accurate conclusion. That position preserves the possibility of future investigation without inflating mystery into proof. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/anomalistics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaAnomalistics - Psi Encyclopedia13 Apr 2015 — Anomalistics is generally governed by the Mertonian norms of science: commun...</span></span></span>

## Why anomalistics matters within Forteana

For Fortean readers, anomalistics offers a practical method rather than a belief system. Charles Fort encouraged attention to observations that established systems ignored or excluded, but anomalistics adds procedures for evaluating those observations instead of merely collecting them.

Its central contribution is methodological rather than ideological. Strange reports become opportunities to test evidence, compare explanations and identify genuine gaps in knowledge. The aim is neither to defend the paranormal nor to explain everything away, but to investigate carefully enough that confidence rises or falls with the quality of the evidence.

In that sense, anomalistics embodies the Fortean habit of curiosity under discipline: remaining interested in anomalies while allowing scientific caution to determine how far any conclusion should go. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/anomalistics/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaAnomalistics - Psi Encyclopedia13 Apr 2015 — Anomalistics is generally governed by the Mertonian norms of science: commun...</span></span></span>
