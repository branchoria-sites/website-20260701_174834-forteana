## Introduction

The Kokomo Hum is one of the few famous hum cases in which investigators identified specific industrial sound sources rather than leaving the phenomenon entirely unexplained. In 2003, an extensive acoustic investigation in Kokomo, Indiana, linked residents' complaints to two distinct low-frequency tones originating from local industrial facilities. Yet the case did not end with that technical success. Even after the identified equipment was modified, some residents continued to report hearing a hum or experiencing related symptoms. That outcome has made Kokomo an important case within Fortean discussions: not because it demonstrates a paranormal mystery, but because it shows that solving part of a problem does not necessarily resolve the entire social and personal [experience]({{ 'experience/' | relative_url }}) surrounding it. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://icben.ethz.ch/2008/PDFs/Cowan.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icben.ethz.ch">[icben.ethz.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icben.ethz.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The results of hum studies in the United States</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">hum study was commissioned in Kokomo, Indiana, where hundreds of residents reported hearing a hum and blamed other, non-acoustical effect...</span></span></span>

## The industrial tones identified in Kokomo

Unlike many reported hums, Kokomo received a detailed engineering investigation commissioned after hundreds of residents complained about a persistent low-frequency noise and associated discomfort. Acoustic consultant James P. Cowan and colleagues monitored sound and vibration throughout the city, compared recordings from multiple neighbourhoods, and traced two recurring tonal signals to specific industrial equipment. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://icben.ethz.ch/2008/PDFs/Cowan.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icben.ethz.ch">[icben.ethz.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icben.ethz.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The results of hum studies in the United States</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">hum study was commissioned in Kokomo, Indiana, where hundreds of residents reported hearing a hum and blamed other, non-acoustical effect...</span></span></span>

The investigation identified two principal sources:

* A **36 Hz** tone generated by a cooling tower on the roof of the DaimlerChrysler casting plant.
* A **10 Hz** tone produced by air compressor intakes at the Haynes International facility.

Rather than relying on witness testimony alone, investigators used directional microphone techniques, spectral analysis and measurements taken progressively closer to the suspected equipment. The 36 Hz tone became stronger as investigators approached the cooling tower, while the 10 Hz tone could similarly be traced to compressor intakes. The measured frequencies matched those recorded in residential areas where complaints had been made. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/297338337/Kokomo-Hum-Report" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Haynes International facility. Daimler Chrysler Casting Plant. We have identified sources of 10 Hz...</span></span></span>

The investigation also examined other suspected industrial sites, including Martin Marietta Aggregates and the Gerber Plumbing Fixtures plant. After on-site inspections and measurements, these facilities were ruled out because investigators could not detect tones matching those heard within affected neighbourhoods. This systematic elimination strengthened confidence that the DaimlerChrysler and Haynes facilities were genuine contributors to at least part of the reported noise problem. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/297338337/Kokomo-Hum-Report" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Haynes International facility. Daimler Chrysler Casting Plant. We have identified sources of 10 Hz...</span></span></span>

Another significant finding was what investigators did **not** discover. Ground vibration measurements remained well below recognised perception thresholds, suggesting that widespread building vibration was unlikely to explain residents' experiences. The evidence therefore pointed primarily towards airborne low-frequency sound rather than seismic vibration. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/297338337/Kokomo-Hum-Report" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Haynes International facility. Daimler Chrysler Casting Plant. We have identified sources of 10 Hz...</span></span></span>

## Why complaints did not neatly end

The Kokomo investigation is frequently misunderstood as proving that the mystery was completely solved. In reality, the engineering study itself anticipated a more complicated outcome.

Its recommendations were cautious. Investigators proposed reducing the identified tones, repeating measurements afterwards, and then determining whether residents still experienced the hum. They explicitly recognised that identifying measurable sound sources was only one part of the overall problem. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/297338337/Kokomo-Hum-Report" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Haynes International facility. Daimler Chrysler Casting Plant. We have identified sources of 10 Hz...</span></span></span>

Subsequent accounts indicate that reports did not disappear entirely after industrial modifications. This persistence became an important feature of later discussions about the Hum. It did not necessarily mean that the original engineering conclusions were wrong. Instead, it suggested several possibilities:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the industrial tones explained only some reports;
* additional low-frequency sources may have existed;
* some listeners may have remained unusually sensitive to environmental sounds;
* prolonged anxiety, sleep disruption or heightened attention may have continued after the loudest sources were reduced.

</div>

The investigators themselves distinguished between **acoustic issues**, which instruments could measure, and **non-acoustic issues**, referring to experiences reported by people even when corresponding airborne sound could not be detected. They did not claim to have identified the cause of every complaint, and they recommended further research rather than declaring the case closed. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/297338337/Kokomo-Hum-Report" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Haynes International facility. Daimler Chrysler Casting Plant. We have identified sources of 10 Hz...</span></span></span>

This distinction is often lost in popular [retellings]({{ 'retellings/' | relative_url }}). Finding real industrial noise did not automatically demonstrate that every reported symptom shared the same origin.

## What Kokomo teaches about mixed causes

Kokomo has become an unusually valuable comparison point because it challenges two overly simple interpretations of hum reports.

The first simplistic view is that every hum must have a mysterious or unknown origin. Kokomo clearly demonstrates that persistent low-frequency industrial tones can travel through communities, be difficult to localise and create genuine disturbance. The engineering investigation identified measurable sources using conventional acoustic methods rather than extraordinary explanations. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/297338337/Kokomo-Hum-Report" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Haynes International facility. Daimler Chrysler Casting Plant. We have identified sources of 10 Hz...</span></span></span>

The opposite simplistic view is that identifying one source completely resolves every complaint. Kokomo does not support that conclusion either. Even the investigators acknowledged that measurable sounds and residents' wider experiences might not correspond perfectly. Some complaints continued after the identified equipment was addressed, leaving open the possibility that multiple mechanisms were operating simultaneously. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/297338337/Kokomo-Hum-Report" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Haynes International facility. Daimler Chrysler Casting Plant. We have identified sources of 10 Hz...</span></span></span>

From a historical perspective, Kokomo therefore occupies an unusual position among famous hum cases. It represents one of the strongest demonstrations that genuine environmental low-frequency noise can underlie community reports, while simultaneously illustrating that technical remediation may not eliminate public uncertainty or personal distress.

## Why Kokomo remains important in Fortean discussions

Within the broader study of unexplained hums, Kokomo is less a solved mystery than a reminder that environmental anomalies can have layered explanations. The case shows that engineering investigations can successfully identify real acoustic sources, yet still leave unanswered questions about why only certain people report hearing them, why symptoms vary between individuals, and why confidence does not necessarily return once a technical intervention has been made. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://icben.ethz.ch/2008/PDFs/Cowan.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icben.ethz.ch">[icben.ethz.ch+2Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icben.ethz.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The results of hum studies in the United States</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">hum study was commissioned in Kokomo, Indiana, where hundreds of residents reported hearing a hum and blamed other, non-acoustical effect...</span></span></span>

For Fortean studies, this makes Kokomo distinctive. Rather than supporting either complete scepticism or extraordinary speculation, it demonstrates how measurable physical phenomena, individual perception, health concerns and community expectations can become intertwined. The enduring significance of the case lies precisely in that incomplete closure: finding a source was an important achievement, but it did not fully resolve the human experience that had turned the hum into a local mystery.
