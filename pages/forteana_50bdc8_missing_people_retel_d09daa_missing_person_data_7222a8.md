## Introduction

Fortean [retellings]({{ 'retellings/' | relative_url }}) often gain their power from the impression that a disappearance is unique, inexplicable or ignored by official systems. In reality, missing-person investigations take place within large national databases that record hundreds of thousands of reports, many of which are resolved quickly while others remain open for years. The United States' National Crime Information Center (NCIC) and the National Missing and Unidentified Persons System (NamUs) do not explain individual mysteries, but they provide an important reality check. They replace anecdote with documented records, show how common missing-person reports actually are, and create opportunities to connect missing people with unidentified remains. At the same time, they reveal that incomplete records and unresolved cases are genuine features of real investigations, not automatic evidence of hidden conspiracies or paranormal causes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://namus.nij.ojp.gov/%3Cfront%3E" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: namus.nij.ojp.gov">[NamUs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">namus.nij.ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NamUs: HomeNamUs is a national centralized repository and resource center for missing, unidentified, and unclaimed person cases acro...</span></span></span>

## What NCIC figures show about scale

The NCIC, operated by the FBI, is the principal national law-enforcement database for missing-person records in the United States. It is not a public storytelling archive but an operational system used by police agencies to enter, update and clear missing-person reports.

For readers encountering Fortean claims that [disappearances]({{ 'disappearances/' | relative_url }}) are unusually frequent in one location or that authorities systematically ignore missing people, NCIC statistics provide essential context. Every year, hundreds of thousands of missing-person records are entered into the database. The overwhelming majority are later cleared when the individual is found or the case is otherwise resolved. For example, the FBI reported that 498,038 missing-person records were entered into NCIC during 2025, while 503,409 records were cleared during the same year. At the end of that year, around 90,000 active records remained. These figures illustrate both the enormous scale of missing-person reporting and the fact that unresolved cases represent only a fraction of annual reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/file-repository/2025-ncic-missing-person-and-unidentified-person-statistics.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2025 ncic missing person and unidentified person statistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2025 NCIC MISSING AND UNIDENTIFIED PERSONFeb 4, 2026 — During 2025, 498,038 missing person records were entered into NCIC. This distinction matters because [folklore]({{ 'folklore/' | relative_url }}) frequently cites the annual number of reports without mentioning how many are resolved. A claim that "hundreds of thousands of people vanish every year" is technically derived from reporting statistics, but it becomes misleading if presented as though all of those people disappeared permanently. NCIC data demonstrate that missing-person reports include a wide range of circumstances, from vulnerable adults and people experiencing mental-health crises to children who quickly return home, alongside the comparatively smaller number of long-term unexplained disappearances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/file-repository/2025-ncic-missing-person-and-unidentified-person-statistics.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2025 ncic missing person and unidentified person statistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2025 NCIC MISSING AND UNIDENTIFIED PERSONFeb 4, 2026 — During 2025, 498,038 missing person records were entered into NCIC. Missing Per...</span></span></span>

Another useful corrective is that NCIC records are administrative rather than narrative. The database does not record whether a disappearance feels mysterious or resembles a folklore pattern. It records legally significant facts that can support investigation, identification and recovery.

## How NamUs helps connect missing and unidentified cases

Where NCIC functions primarily as a law-enforcement information system, NamUs was designed to solve one of the biggest practical problems in missing-person investigations: connecting separate records that may describe the same individual.

NamUs is a national repository supported through the U.S. Department of Justice that contains missing-person cases, unidentified human remains and unclaimed-person records. Unlike NCIC alone, it integrates forensic information such as dental records, fingerprints, DNA profiles, anthropological analysis and photographs where available. It also provides analytical tools that help investigators compare missing persons with unidentified remains across jurisdictional boundaries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://namus.nij.ojp.gov/%3Cfront%3E" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: namus.nij.ojp.gov">[NamUs+2NamUs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">namus.nij.ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NamUs: HomeNamUs is a national centralized repository and resource center for missing, unidentified, and unclaimed person cases acro...</span></span></span>

This capability has direct relevance to Fortean retellings. Many mystery narratives assume that a vanished person simply "disappeared without trace." In practice, unidentified remains may exist in another county or state, under a different case number and investigated by a different agency. NamUs exists precisely because such fragmented record-keeping has historically prevented cases from being linked.

Since its creation, NamUs has assisted in tens of thousands of case resolutions and facilitated thousands of identifications through forensic services including DNA comparison, fingerprint examination, forensic odontology and, more recently, forensic genetic genealogy. These successes illustrate that apparently impossible disappearances are sometimes resolved through improved data sharing rather than dramatic new evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rti.org/impact/namus-national-missing-unidentified-persons-system" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rti.org">[RTI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rti.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">namus national missing unidentified persons system</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NAMUS: Forensic Genealogy &amp; Missing Persons StatisticsJun 19, 2024 — NamUs currently houses records for more than 54,000 active missin...</span></span></span>

For readers interested in [Forteana]({{ 'forteana/' | relative_url }}), this is an important shift in perspective. A mystery can persist for decades not because it is supernatural but because records, biological evidence and investigative jurisdictions remained disconnected until new databases or forensic methods brought them together.

## Why databases still leave room for uncertainty

Neither NCIC nor NamUs should be treated as complete or infallible records of every disappearance.

NCIC depends on information entered and maintained by law-enforcement agencies. NamUs also relies on agencies and authorised users to submit and update cases. Although some U.S. states require eligible cases to be entered into NamUs, nationwide participation has historically varied, meaning that not every missing person or unidentified decedent appears in the public-facing system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://namus.nij.ojp.gov/%3Cfront%3E" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: namus.nij.ojp.gov">[NamUs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">namus.nij.ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NamUs: HomeNamUs is a national centralized repository and resource center for missing, unidentified, and unclaimed person cases acro...</span></span></span>

The databases also serve different purposes. NCIC includes operational records that are not all publicly accessible, whereas NamUs selectively publishes unresolved cases and supports investigations with forensic services. Consequently, the absence of a case from NamUs should not automatically be interpreted as evidence that authorities ignored it or concealed information.

Equally, an unresolved listing does not imply paranormal circumstances. It may reflect missing biological evidence, unidentified remains lacking usable DNA, incomplete historical documentation, jurisdictional complexity or the simple fact that no confirmed lead has yet emerged. Modern forensic techniques continue to resolve cases that remained open for decades, demonstrating that uncertainty often reflects investigative limits rather than inexplicable events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rti.org/impact/namus-national-missing-unidentified-persons-system" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rti.org">[RTI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rti.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">namus national missing unidentified persons system</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NAMUS: Forensic Genealogy &amp; Missing Persons StatisticsJun 19, 2024 — NamUs currently houses records for more than 54,000 active missin...</span></span></span>

## Reading mystery stories alongside official records

For anyone examining missing-person folklore, NCIC and NamUs are best understood as baseline reference points rather than final authorities.

Before accepting claims that a disappearance was uniquely mysterious, several questions become useful:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Was the individual officially reported missing and entered into national systems?<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.reveilleadvisors.com/national-missing-persons-database/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reveilleadvisors.com">[reveilleadvisors.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reveilleadvisors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">national missing persons database</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NamUs helps report and find missing persons and unidentified remains...</span></span></span>
* Does the case remain active, or was it later resolved?
* Has it been compared against unidentified human remains through forensic databases?
* Are later retellings adding details that do not appear in documented investigative records?
* Is the apparent mystery created by missing evidence, or by missing context?

</div>

These questions do not diminish genuine mysteries. Instead, they separate documented uncertainty from narrative embellishment. Fortean retellings often thrive on memorable anomalies, whereas databases emphasise verification, correction and comparison. The tension between those two approaches explains why official records rarely make compelling legends, yet they remain indispensable for judging whether a legend reflects an unresolved investigation or simply the evolution of folklore.
