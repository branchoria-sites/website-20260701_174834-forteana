## Introduction

Many reported poltergeist cases begin with a simple [observation]({{ 'observation/' | relative_url }}): unexplained knocking, creaking, footsteps overhead or sharp bangs somewhere inside an ordinary home. Because these sounds often occur without an obvious source, particularly at night, they can become the first "evidence" that a house is haunted. In practice, however, buildings are complex mechanical systems. Timber expands and contracts, plumbing changes pressure, heating systems cycle on and off, appliances vibrate, and animals move through hidden spaces. Once a supernatural explanation takes hold, ordinary noises may be reinterpreted as part of an escalating pattern rather than as unrelated household events.

This does not mean every unexplained sound has an immediate explanation. Rather, investigations into alleged hauntings consistently show that domestic noises deserve careful examination before they are treated as evidence of paranormal activity. The challenge is not that houses are silent until [ghosts]({{ 'ghosts/' | relative_url }}) arrive; it is that occupied buildings produce an astonishing variety of sounds that are easily misidentified under conditions of uncertainty.

## Common domestic sources of knocks and bangs

Most houses generate intermittent noises without any fault or supernatural cause. Many occur only under particular temperature, [weather]({{ 'weather/' | relative_url }}) or occupancy conditions, making them seem mysterious because they resist easy repetition.

Some of the most common mechanisms include:

* **Heating pipes and plumbing.** Hot water causes metal pipes to expand against joists, brackets and wall cavities. As they cool, they contract again. This can produce loud single knocks, rapid tapping or sequences of bangs that appear to move through the building. Water hammer—pressure waves created when valves close suddenly—can create particularly sharp impacts.
* **Floorboards and timber framing.** Wood absorbs and releases moisture, expanding in humid conditions and shrinking in dry ones. Seasonal changes or overnight temperature drops can make floorboards, roof trusses and wall studs creak or crack loudly.
* **Central heating and boilers.** Boilers, radiators and ductwork cycle automatically. Metal ducts flex as warm air passes through them, while pumps and valves generate intermittent clicks and vibrations.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Appliances and vibration.** Washing machines, refrigerators, freezers, extractors and air-conditioning equipment can transmit vibrations into floors and walls, making sounds appear to originate somewhere entirely different.
* **Roof spaces and wall cavities.** Birds, rodents, squirrels and other animals often move through lofts or cavity walls. Scratching, thumping or brief running sounds can easily resemble footsteps, especially when the exact location is hidden.
* **Wind and weather.** Loose roof tiles, gutters, fencing, branches, chimneys and exterior cladding all respond to changing wind conditions. Because gusts vary unpredictably, the resulting noises may seem intelligent or selective.

</div>

Buildings also conduct sound surprisingly well. A knock produced several rooms away may emerge from a wall or ceiling because timber framing, metal pipework and masonry transmit vibration more efficiently than air. As a result, witnesses often misjudge both the direction and distance of the original sound.

## Why night-time settings distort judgement

The classic "bump in the night" is not simply a cultural cliché. Several well-understood psychological and environmental factors make nocturnal sounds more likely to be interpreted as meaningful.

At night, background traffic, conversation and household activity decline. The relative quiet makes ordinary structural noises far more noticeable than during the day. Sleepiness also reduces attention and increases uncertainty about what has just been heard.

Darkness removes visual information that normally helps identify a sound's source. A radiator settling after heating may sound remarkably like footsteps when the listener cannot simultaneously see that nothing is moving.

Expectation amplifies the effect. Once residents begin discussing possible paranormal activity, confirmation bias encourages them to notice every unusual creak while overlooking the hundreds of ordinary noises that occur without incident. Investigators of alleged hauntings have repeatedly noted that expectation changes how ambiguous events are interpreted, particularly when witnesses already know a property's [haunted reputation]({{ 'place-reputation/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haunted_house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Haunted house</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Haunted house</span></span></span>

Emotional states matter as well. Stress, bereavement, anxiety, conflict within a household and prolonged sleep disruption can increase vigilance, making ambiguous sounds seem unusually significant without implying dishonesty on the part of witnesses.

## When vibration feels like a presence

Not every unsettling [experience]({{ 'experience/' | relative_url }}) involves an audible bang. Some buildings contain very low-frequency vibrations, known as infrasound, generated by ventilation systems, boilers, traffic or large mechanical equipment.

Recent laboratory research found that exposure to infrasound increased irritation and physiological stress, even when participants could not consciously hear it. Researchers argue that these subtle bodily sensations may contribute to reports that an old basement or house "feels wrong" before any obvious noise is noticed. The distinction is important. Feeling watched, uncomfortable or tense inside an old building may have an environmental component without providing evidence for a poltergeist.

## Why ordinary noises become convincing evidence

Domestic sounds rarely remain isolated. Instead, witnesses often assemble them into a narrative.

A sequence might develop like this:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. A loud unexplained bang wakes someone during the night.
2. Days later, another unrelated noise occurs elsewhere in the house.
3. An object is discovered where nobody remembers leaving it.
4. Family members begin discussing whether the events are connected.
5. Future noises are interpreted through that new framework.

</div>

Humans are exceptionally good at detecting patterns, even when events are unrelated. Once separate incidents become linked into a single story, the perceived strength of the haunting increases despite each individual event having an [ordinary explanation]({{ 'ordinary-causes/' | relative_url }}).

This process explains why many famous poltergeist investigations devote considerable effort to separating genuinely connected events from the normal background activity of occupied homes.

## Checks that separate physics from mystery

Investigators—whether sceptical or sympathetic to paranormal claims—often begin with practical environmental checks before considering more unusual explanations.

Useful steps include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Recording exactly when noises occur and whether they correlate with heating cycles, plumbing use or weather.
* Checking lofts, crawl spaces and wall cavities for wildlife.
* Inspecting loose pipe brackets, radiators, ductwork and floorboards.
* Comparing sounds heard inside with those produced outside by neighbouring properties, traffic or industrial equipment.
* Determining whether multiple independent listeners identify the same source location or whether perceptions differ.

</div>

Controlled observation is especially valuable because many supposed "mysterious" noises disappear once their physical origin is identified. Equally important, if sounds continue after common causes have been ruled out, investigators can define the remaining problem more precisely rather than beginning with a paranormal assumption.

## Why houses are effective "evidence machines"

Ordinary homes unintentionally create ideal conditions for mistaken [poltergeist reports]({{ 'poltergeists-edffb6/' | relative_url }}). They contain concealed spaces, changing temperatures, moving water, flexible materials, hidden animals and countless sources of intermittent vibration. Their internal structure also makes locating sounds unexpectedly difficult.

For Fortean researchers, this does not eliminate the possibility of unusual cases. Instead, it explains why knocks, bangs and footsteps alone carry limited evidential weight. Household noises are among the weakest forms of evidence for a poltergeist precisely because they arise so readily from normal building behaviour. Only after these familiar mechanisms have been carefully examined can any remaining unexplained events be assessed on their own merits.

    if (localeMarkets.language && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.language) >= 0) return localeMarkets.language;
