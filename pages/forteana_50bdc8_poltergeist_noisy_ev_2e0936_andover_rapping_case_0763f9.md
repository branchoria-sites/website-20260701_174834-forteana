## Introduction

The Andover rapping case is often cited as one of the more intriguing modern British poltergeist investigations because the central claim was not merely that unexplained knocks occurred, but that the knocks appeared to answer questions intelligently. If genuine, this would move the evidence beyond unexplained noise towards apparent communication. Yet this same feature also creates the case's greatest evidential weakness. Determining whether a sequence of knocks genuinely conveys intention is far more difficult than establishing that sounds occurred at all. The Andover investigation therefore occupies an important place in discussions of poltergeist evidence: it illustrates how reports of "meaningful" responses can simultaneously strengthen a paranormal claim and make it more vulnerable to alternative interpretations. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/andover-case-responsive-rapping-poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Andover Case: A Responsive Rapping PoltergeistAfter the fourth rap, I continued to hold the card in place in case any further raps we...</span></span></span>

## Responsive rapping in the Hampshire house

The reported events took place in a council house in Andover, Hampshire, during the spring of 1974. Barrie G. Colvin investigated the family over a series of visits after local newspaper reports drew attention to persistent rapping sounds. According to his later published account, the household had gradually developed a simple code by which knocks could represent answers to questions, allowing apparently meaningful exchanges with an unseen source. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/andover-case-responsive-rapping-poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Andover Case: A Responsive Rapping PoltergeistAfter the fourth rap, I continued to hold the card in place in case any further raps we...</span></span></span>

Unlike many famous poltergeist cases, the principal claim was not that furniture flew across rooms or objects were violently displaced. Instead, the remarkable feature was the alleged responsiveness of the sounds. Witnesses reported that knocks came from walls, bedheads and, at times, other nearby objects, with the number or sequence of raps corresponding to requested answers. This focus on communication makes Andover unusual even within the already specialised category of "rapping" [poltergeist reports]({{ 'poltergeists-edffb6/' | relative_url }}). <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/andover-case-responsive-rapping-poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Andover Case: A Responsive Rapping PoltergeistAfter the fourth rap, I continued to hold the card in place in case any further raps we...</span></span></span>

Colvin regarded the apparent intelligence of the responses as the defining characteristic of the case. In one reported experiment, a concealed number was presented while another family member was asked to indicate it solely through raps. According to the published report, the correct number of knocks was produced repeatedly under those test conditions. Such episodes formed the strongest evidential core of his investigation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/andover-case-responsive-rapping-poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Andover Case: A Responsive Rapping PoltergeistAfter the fourth rap, I continued to hold the card in place in case any further raps we...</span></span></span>

## Controls used to exclude ordinary causes

Because ordinary knocking can be produced by people, buildings or hidden mechanical means, Colvin attempted a variety of informal controls intended to reduce obvious normal explanations.

These included:

* observing walls and furniture closely while raps occurred;
* attempting to determine whether sounds shifted between different physical objects;
* varying who asked questions;
* introducing simple tests in which the expected response was not obvious from casual [observation]({{ 'observation/' | relative_url }});
* recording detailed notes over repeated visits rather than relying on a single dramatic incident. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/andover-case-responsive-rapping-poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Andover Case: A Responsive Rapping PoltergeistAfter the fourth rap, I continued to hold the card in place in case any further raps we...</span></span></span>

One aspect frequently highlighted by proponents is the reported transfer of the apparent sound source. Colvin described occasions when raps seemed to originate not only from walls but also from objects under direct observation, making hidden manual production appear less straightforward than if every sound had come from a single fixed location. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/andover-case-responsive-rapping-poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Andover Case: A Responsive Rapping PoltergeistAfter the fourth rap, I continued to hold the card in place in case any further raps we...</span></span></span>

Even so, these controls fell well short of modern experimental standards. The investigation occurred in an occupied family home rather than a laboratory. Continuous audio recording, multiple independent observers, blind protocols and comprehensive physical monitoring were not available throughout the reported events. As a result, the controls could reduce some conventional explanations without conclusively eliminating them. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/andover-case-responsive-rapping-poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Andover Case: A Responsive Rapping PoltergeistAfter the fourth rap, I continued to hold the card in place in case any further raps we...</span></span></span>

## Why intention is difficult to prove from sound

The central problem raised by the Andover case is philosophical as much as investigative: when does a sequence of knocks become evidence of intention rather than coincidence?

Several difficulties arise.

**Pattern recognition.** Humans are exceptionally good at finding meaning in ambiguous events. Once a simple code exists, listeners naturally interpret uncertain sounds as fitting expected answers, particularly if they already believe communication may be occurring.

**Flexible questioning.** Many questions posed during alleged rapping sessions permit broad interpretation. Slight uncertainty over when counting begins or ends can change the apparent message without anyone deliberately manipulating the outcome.

**Observer expectation.** Investigators and witnesses who hope for meaningful responses may unintentionally reinforce a communication system by asking leading questions, repeating unclear responses or accepting approximate answers.

**Limited information content.** A series of knocks generally conveys very little information. A correct "yes", "no" or small number may seem impressive, but statistically such responses require relatively little information to match expectations compared with longer, independently verifiable messages.

These problems do not demonstrate fraud or error in any particular instance. Rather, they explain why responsive rapping is inherently more difficult to evaluate than it first appears. Apparent intelligence can emerge from surprisingly small amounts of ambiguous information. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/andover-case-responsive-rapping-poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Andover Case: A Responsive Rapping PoltergeistAfter the fourth rap, I continued to hold the card in place in case any further raps we...</span></span></span>

## Why the case remains debated

Supporters argue that Andover deserves attention because the observations extended across multiple visits and included attempts, however limited, to test the responsiveness of the phenomena rather than merely collecting anecdotes. They also point to similarities with historical responsive-rapping cases documented in psychical research literature, suggesting that Andover fits a recurring pattern rather than standing alone. spr.ac.uk+2Southwest Ghost Hunters Association <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/andover-case-responsive-rapping-poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Andover Case: A Responsive Rapping PoltergeistAfter the fourth rap, I continued to hold the card in place in case any further raps we...</span></span></span>

Sceptics reach a different conclusion. They note that the strongest evidence depends heavily on witness testimony collected by a single principal investigator and lacks the level of independent replication expected for extraordinary claims. ## What Andover contributes to the study of noisy evidence

Within the broader study of poltergeist reports, the Andover case is valuable less because it settles the existence of paranormal communication than because it exposes a recurring evidential dilemma. A loud unexplained bang may indicate only an unexplained noise. A bang that seems to answer a question appears more impressive, yet it also introduces new interpretive problems concerning language, expectation and probability.

For that reason, Andover continues to be discussed as a classic example of the difference between demonstrating that unusual sounds occurred and demonstrating that those sounds expressed an independent intelligence.
